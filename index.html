01 — The idea

When infrastructure fails, people still need a toilet.

Floods, earthquakes, camps, festivals, mountain refuges. Sanitation is often the first thing to break and the last to arrive. habitual brings it with you, in a box.

/01

No sewer.

Nothing to dig, connect or pump. It stands on its own wherever you set it down.

/02

No water.

A urine-diverting seat and a handful of sawdust replace the flush, and the smell.

/03

No waste.

What goes in becomes compost and fertilizer instead of blackwater.

05 — How it works

Simpler than a flush.

  1. 1

    Use it like any toilet.

    Same seat, same position for everyone. The separator keeps urine and solids apart, so nothing ferments and nothing smells.

  2. 2

    Cover with a cup.

    Add about 250 ml of sawdust, ash, dry soil or rice husks. It absorbs moisture and neutralizes odour.

    250 ml
  3. 3

    Seal and rest.

    When the container is full, close it airtight from the back and let it rest for at least six months.

    6 months
  4. 4

    Back to the earth.

    After about 24 months in a closed composter it becomes humus for the garden. Diluted urine works as liquid fertilizer.

    24 months

08 — Good questions

Curious? Good.

All questions →
Does it smell?

No. Odours only appear when urine and solids mix. The separator keeps them apart and the absorbent material handles the rest.

Does it need water or electricity?

No. It works completely off-grid, with no plumbing, no sewer and no power. The Mini arrives flat-packed and goes up in minutes.

Is it hygienic?

Yes. You only touch the seat, as with any toilet. The containers are airtight and leak-proof, so they can be moved without spills.

Will I see the waste?

No. The container is black and light doesn't reach it. Because paper and absorbent go in last, you only ever see the covering material.

Do I need a building permit?

Usually not for outdoor models: the system produces no leachate or blackwater. It's still worth checking with your local municipality.
